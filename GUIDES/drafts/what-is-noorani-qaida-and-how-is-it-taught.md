## H1: What Is Noorani Qaida and How Is It Taught?

**Intro:**
Every parent searching for Quran classes hears the same term: *Noorani Qaida*. But what actually is it? Think of it as the alphabet book of the Quran — a small, carefully ordered primer that takes a complete beginner from "I don't know a single Arabic letter" to "I can read words of the Quran with correct pronunciation." It is the universal first step: nearly every child who learns to read the Quran starts here.

This guide explains what Noorani Qaida covers, how qualified teachers teach it online, how long it takes, and what comes after.

---

## The short answer

**Noorani Qaida is a beginner's primer that teaches the Arabic alphabet, the correct pronunciation of each letter, and the rules for joining letters into words.** It is the foundation of all Quran reading: you cannot read the Quran correctly without the skills the Qaida builds. Online, it is taught live and one-on-one — a tutor listens to your child read each lesson, corrects pronunciation on the spot, and only moves forward when the current step is solid.

---

## What Noorani Qaida actually covers

The Qaida is short, but every page earns its place. In order, it teaches:

- **The Arabic alphabet** — all 29 letters, in their isolated forms, with their names.
- **Makharij (pronunciation points)** — exactly where in the mouth, throat, or lips each letter's sound is produced. This is the heart of correct recitation.
- **Harakat** — the short vowels (fatha, kasra, damma) that give letters their sounds.
- **Tanween** — the "double vowels" (un, an, in).
- **Joining letters** — how letters change shape at the beginning, middle, and end of words, and how to read connected words smoothly.
- **Sukoon and Tashdeed** — silent letters and doubled/emphasized letters.
- **Madd (elongation)** — the long vowels and stretching rules.
- **Basic Tajweed foundations** — early rules like Noon Sakinah, so the student enters Quran reading with correct habits, not mistakes to unlearn later.

By the final pages, a student is reading full words and short phrases — genuinely reading, not memorizing shapes.

## Why every beginner starts here (and not straight in the Quran)

It is tempting to skip ahead. Don't. The Qaida exists because jumping straight into the Quran builds bad pronunciation habits that take years to fix. A child who rushes learns to *guess* words; a child who finishes the Qaida learns to *read* them.

The Prophet ﷺ said: **"The best of you are those who learn the Quran and teach it."** (Sahih al-Bukhari) — and learning it *correctly* starts with this foundation. A slow, correct start beats a fast, sloppy one every time.

## How Noorani Qaida is taught online

The online method is the same proven method used in masjids for generations — delivered over live video:

1. **The tutor shares the Qaida page on screen.** Your child sees the exact lesson, large and clear.
2. **The tutor reads, the child repeats.** Letter by letter, sound by sound — the tutor demonstrates the correct Makhraj and the child imitates it.
3. **Instant correction.** This is what makes one-on-one essential: the tutor hears *your* child's pronunciation and fixes it immediately. In a group, a wrong sound can hide for months.
4. **Sabaq (new lesson) + Sabqi (revision).** Each class introduces a small new portion and revises recent ones — the classical system that makes learning stick.
5. **No advancement without mastery.** A good tutor never moves to joining rules while the alphabet is still shaky. The class moves at your child's pace, not a syllabus deadline.

Classes are typically 30 minutes, 3–5 days a week. Short, consistent sessions beat long, irregular ones — especially for young children.

## How long does it take?

For a child practicing consistently: **about 3–4 months** to complete the Qaida with correct pronunciation. Adults often move faster. These are honest averages, not promises — the tutor's rule is simple: finish when it's *correct*, not when the calendar says so. A rushed Qaida is a wasted Qaida.

---

## What comes after the Qaida

Completing Noorani Qaida is the doorway, not the destination:

1. **Nazra** — reading the full Quran fluently from the page, applying everything learned.
2. **Tajweed** — the full rules of beautiful, correct recitation.
3. **Hifz** — memorization, for those who choose it.

Each stage builds on the last, and the Qaida is what makes all of them possible.

## FAQ

**What age should a child start Noorani Qaida?**
Most children are ready between ages 4 and 6 — when they can sit for short periods and enjoy rhymes and repetition. Our free 3-day trial doubles as a readiness check: the tutor will tell you honestly if waiting a few months is better.

**Can an adult learn Noorani Qaida online?**
Yes — adults and reverts start here too, and often progress faster than children because they understand the *why* behind each rule.

**Does my child need to know Arabic first?**
No. The Qaida assumes zero knowledge — it *is* the first step.

**How do I know the teacher is teaching it correctly?**
The sign is correction: a good tutor stops your child on mispronounced letters and drills the Makhraj until it's right. If a class moves through pages with no corrections, that's a red flag.
